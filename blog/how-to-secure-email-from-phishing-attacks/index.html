How to Secure Your Small Business’s Email from Phishing Attacks

June 17, 2026· 2 min read

Phishing attacks are a serious threat for small businesses, often leading to data breaches or financial losses. By securing your email systems, you can significantly reduce the risk of falling victim to these scams. Here’s how to do it:

Understand What Phishing Is

Phishing is when attackers send fake emails that look like they come from legitimate sources, such as banks or HR departments. Their goal is to trick you into revealing sensitive information like passwords or credit card numbers.

Train Your Team

  • Organize regular training sessions on recognizing phishing emails. Teach employees to look for signs of phishing, like suspicious URLs, poor grammar, and urgent calls to action.

  • Encourage a culture where employees feel comfortable reporting suspected phishing attempts. This can help you quickly identify and address potential issues before they cause damage.

Use Strong Passwords and MFA

  • Ensure all email accounts use strong, unique passwords. Avoid common words or easily guessable information like birthdays or pet names.

  • Implement Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) to add an extra layer of security. This requires users to provide two or more verification factors before accessing their accounts.

Enable Email Filters and Security Tools

  • Use email filters that can automatically detect and block suspicious messages. Most major email providers offer built-in filtering options.

  • Consider using security tools like phishing protection software or advanced spam filters to further secure your business’s email inboxes.

Keep Software Updated

Maintain up-to-date versions of all your software, including your email client. Regular updates often include patches that address known vulnerabilities.

Set Up Two-Factor Authentication for Critical Services

  • Enable 2FA for any critical services your business uses, such as online banking or cloud storage. This adds another layer of security beyond just passwords.

  • Make sure you or your team members are aware of the two-factor authentication process and how to set it up.

Regularly Review Email Policies

Review and update your company’s email policies regularly. Make sure they cover all aspects of secure email use, from password management to phishing detection.

Stay Informed and Vigilant

The threat landscape is constantly changing, so staying informed about the latest phishing tactics is crucial. Regularly check security bulletins and updates for your company’s IT systems.
