How to Secure Your Small Business’s Email from Phishing Attacks

June 20, 2026· 2 min read

Phishing attacks are a significant threat to any small business, posing risks from both external hackers and even within the organization. These scams can lead to data breaches, financial losses, and damaged reputations. By understanding what phishing is and how to prevent it, you can protect your small business's sensitive information.

What is Phishing?

Phishing occurs when cybercriminals send fraudulent emails that appear legitimate, tricking recipients into revealing personal or financial information. These emails often mimic official-looking messages from banks, social media sites, and other well-known companies. The goal is to steal your login credentials, credit card numbers, or even company secrets.

How to Spot a Phishing Email

  • Check the Sender’s Address: Genuine emails from reputable companies will have official email addresses that end in .com or .org. Look out for misspellings or suspicious domains like @example.com.
  • Watch for Urgent Requests: Phishers often create a sense of urgency by stating there’s an urgent action required, such as changing your password immediately.
  • Be Skeptical of Attachments and Links: Do not open attachments or click on links in suspicious emails. They may contain malware that can infect your computer or network.

Steps to Protect Your Business from Phishing

  • Train Your Staff: Regularly educate employees about phishing and how to recognize it. Conduct mock phishing tests to ensure they stay alert.
  • Implement Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Enable MFA on all business email accounts, especially for those with access to sensitive information.
  • Use Email Filters: Configure your email client or use a service that filters out known phishing emails based on their content and sender details.
  • Keep Software Updated: Ensure all software, including email clients, is up-to-date to protect against the latest security threats.
