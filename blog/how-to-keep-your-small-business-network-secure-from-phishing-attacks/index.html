How to Keep Your Small Business Network Secure from Phishing Attacks

June 17, 2026· 2 min read

Phishing attacks are a common threat faced by businesses of all sizes, including small offices. These sophisticated scams can trick even the most cautious employees into revealing sensitive information or downloading harmful malware onto their devices.

What is Phishing?

Phishing is when cybercriminals send emails or messages that look legitimate and ask you to provide personal information like passwords, bank details, or other confidential data. They often pose as trusted organizations such as banks, tech companies, or government agencies to gain your trust.

Risk Factors in Small Businesses

  • Small teams may not have dedicated IT staff, leading to less frequent security updates and training.

  • New employees might be less aware of phishing risks due to a lack of formal cybersecurity training.

  • The remote work trend has increased the attack surface for phishing as employees use multiple devices and networks outside the office.

Protecting Your Business from Phishing

  1. Stay Informed: Regularly update yourself on the latest phishing tactics. Common indicators include suspicious sender addresses, urgent or threatening language, and links or attachments that look fishy.

  2. Train Your Team: Implement regular security awareness training to educate employees about recognizing phishing attempts. This can be as simple as a monthly newsletter highlighting recent scams.

  3. Use Phishing Filters: Most email providers offer built-in spam filters and anti-phishing features that can help identify suspicious messages before they reach your inbox.

  4. Verify Information: If an email asks for sensitive information, contact the supposed sender via a known phone number or company website to confirm its legitimacy.

Additional Security Measures

  • Implement multi-factor authentication (MFA) for critical applications and services.

  • Use a reputable antivirus software that can protect against phishing links and attachments.

  • Keep your operating system and other software up to date with the latest security patches.

Conclusion and Call to Action

Phishing attacks are an ongoing threat, but by staying informed and implementing these simple steps, you can significantly reduce the risk of falling victim.
